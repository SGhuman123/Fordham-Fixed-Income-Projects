# The Return of the Loan: Direct Mortgage Lending vs. a CMBS D-Bond

Group case study for Fixed Income Securities (QFGB-8931), Fordham University, Spring 2026.

## The question

An investor has $5.8 million to put into commercial real estate debt. It can either lend directly
in commercial mortgages or buy the subordinated **D-bond** of a new six-loan CMBS deal. The case
asks:

1. Build the promised cash flows of direct mortgage lending (Exhibit 6) and estimate its expected
   return.
2. Identify the adverse economic scenario most likely to cause a CMBS loan to default, assign it a
   probability, and derive the expected return on the affected loan and the expected cash flows to
   the D-bond.
3. Set a required return for the D-bond and find the price (relative to par), and the face value
   that $5.8 million buys, at which the D-bond is preferable to direct lending.

## Approach and results

**Q1: Direct lending.** A $5.8M, 8.0% loan with 30-year amortization, a 5-year balloon and a
100 bp origination fee. Net outlay is $5.742M, the monthly payment is $42,558 and the balloon is
$5.514M. The promised IRR is **8.57%**. After deducting an annualized credit loss built from
the Ba 5-year cumulative default/loss rate (7.02%, Exhibit 15) and a 40% recovery, the expected return is
**7.12%**, about 72 bp over the 6.40% Baa corporate yield.

**Q2: Adverse scenario.** South Plains Mall, the largest loan in the pool ($77.7M at 4.27%),
loses both anchors, Dillard's and JC Penney (about 47% of NRA), and the co-tenancy clauses
cut a further 50% of the remaining NOI. Stressed NOI falls to $6.0M, the stressed value at a 10%
cap rate is $60.3M, and the loan defaults in month 30 with a $14.0M loss. Because the D-bond has
no subordination beneath it, it absorbs the whole loss. With a 15% scenario probability, the
D-bond's expected IRR when bought at par is **3.17%**, against a 4.76% promised yield.

**Q3: Pricing the D-bond.** The required return is built up as the 6.40% Baa yield, plus 50 bp
for illiquidity (Rule 144A), plus 75 bp for concentration in a six-loan pool, giving **7.65%**.
This beats the 7.12% from direct lending, so it is the hurdle. Discounting the D-bond's expected
cash flows at 7.65% gives a break-even price of **83.44% of par**. At that price $5.8M buys
**$6.95M of face**, and the promised yield is 9.13%. At par the D-bond is inferior to direct
lending. At about 83.4 or below it is the better use of the capital.
